## fisher_results_01 (43s)
Hook: END YOUR STORY WITH RESULTS
Take: Name the outcome, even a small one. A story needs an ending.
End: What was your last result?
Caption: Telling an interview story and forgetting the result is like a great movie with no ending. A Fisher College interviewer's tip: use situation, task, action, result, and always say how it ended.

## fisher_iwe_02 (32s)
Hook: SAY I, NOT WE, IN INTERVIEWS
Take: Say what you personally did. Cut the we from your story.
End: Follow for a clip a day
Caption: Interviewers want to hear what you did, not what the committee did. Fisher College's advice: say "I", give the result, and add names, dates, numbers and places.
Hashtags: #InterviewTips #JobInterview #CareerAdvice #Hiring #JobSearch #Reels

## fisher_stories_03 (32s)
Hook: PREP STORIES FROM OLD EMAILS
Take: Scan old emails and projects for 2 or 3 stories you remember well.
End: Which story would you tell?
Caption: How to prepare for behavioral questions: look through your resume and the last few months of emails, then pick two or three stories you can retell in detail, including the options you weighed. Tip from Fisher College of Business.
Hashtags: #InterviewPrep #JobInterview #CareerAdvice #InterviewTips #JobSearch #Reels

## fisher_redpen_04 (49s)
Hook: 12 HOURS OF WORK GOT AN X
Take: A failure story works when you say what happened and what you did.
End: Follow for a clip a day
Caption: A Fisher College example answer: an intern spent 12 hours on a merger model and had it crossed out with a red pen in week one. A real story with a setback is what the "tell me about a time you fell short" question is asking for.
Hashtags: #InterviewTips #Internship #CareerAdvice #JobInterview #FirstJob #Reels

## fisher_effort_05 (56s)
Hook: EFFORT BEATS PERFECT
Take: In a new job, show effort and learn fast. Nobody expects it all.
End: Ever failed at a first task?
Caption: The end of the intern's story: his work was wrong, but his senior colleague later said his determination stood out, and he got a return offer. Employers know new people do not know everything and look at effort. From Fisher College of Business.

## fisherb_reasons_01 (34s)
Hook: 3 REASONS, EACH WITH PROOF
Take: Pick three reasons to hire you. Back each with a short example.
End: Follow for a clip a day
Caption: "Why should we hire you?" Fisher College's tip: choose three reasons, tie each to the job description, and back each with a brief example, like being organized.
